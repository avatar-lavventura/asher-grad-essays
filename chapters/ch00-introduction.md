# Introduction: How to Use This Book

## Core Idea
Read the entire book first, then restart completing each assignment in order before moving to the next chapter. Do not write a single word until you have done the self-assessment exercises in Chapter 5.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Read-First, Write-Second Protocol**: Complete the whole book passively, then loop back actively — assignments must be done in sequence
  - When to use: Before any essay drafting begins
  - How: Full read → complete Chapter 5 exercises → start drafting
- **Procrastination Recovery Sequence**: If you are out of time, read Chapters 4, 5, 6, and 7 in order; do assignments as you go; do not read ahead
  - When to use: Last-minute applicants (you can finish a good essay by tomorrow morning)

## Key Concepts
- **Essay as Process**: The book's approach is iterative — pool of data → first draft → multiple subsequent drafts; never a single sitting
- **Safety Schools**: If you have backup options, you need not follow every technique; take what applies
- **Three unique features**: (1) full chapter on letters of recommendation, (2) scholarship/fellowship/residency essay samples, (3) overview of entire application ecosystem

## Mental Models
- Think of yourself as smarter than you've been told — years of professor feedback on errors underestimates your real ability
- This is a study of the ideal way to apply — not a recipe to follow slavishly; adapt to your situation
- "You can make a good graduate application" even without changing who you are or what you've done

## Anti-patterns
- **Writing before doing Chapter 5**: The prewriting exercises are what produce the pool of material; skipping them leads to generic essays
- **Reading only your field's sections**: The book instructs reading all sections, even those that don't apply directly
- **Submitting first-choice application first**: Finish three complete applications before submitting the one that matters most

## Key Takeaways
1. Do not write until you have completed the Chapter 5 self-assessment exercises.
2. The ideal sequence: read whole book, then redo assignments chapter by chapter.
3. For competitive programs the technique matters; for less competitive ones, take what's useful.
4. Finish three applications before submitting your first-choice application.
