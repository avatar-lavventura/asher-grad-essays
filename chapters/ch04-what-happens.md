# Chapter 4: What Happens to Your Application

## Core Idea
Once you submit, your application enters an institutional structure with multiple layers of authority; understanding that structure helps you manage what you can still influence from the outside.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Two Admissions Authority Models**:
  - Strong Graduate School (Duke model): Graduate school sets binding standards, can override departments. Apply to both the graduate school and the department.
  - Strong Department (Harvard model): Graduate school is administrative; departments control admission decisions. Faculty in the department are the real decision-makers.
  - Professional schools (business, law, medicine): Admissions staff (including rotating faculty) hold authority.
- **Dual-Format Submission Rule**: For any supplementary material (writing samples, research papers, publications) where online submission is unclear, send both electronically AND by paper/mail directly to anyone likely on the admissions committee. A note: "I did not see a failsafe submission method online, and I wanted you to have this."
- **Completeness Is Your Responsibility**: The online system saying "complete" is unreliable. Track each letter of recommendation personally, and confirm with the admissions office that everything is logged.

## Key Concepts
- **Draft Visibility**: When filling in essay windows online, draft elsewhere and paste only your final version. In most systems, admissions staff can view your drafts if they choose to.
- **Character/Word Count Traps**: Some windows truncate content that exceeds the limit without warning. Verify the full essay is readable in the submission window after pasting.
- **Formatting Conversion Issues**: Apostrophes, quote marks, dashes, scientific notation can corrupt when pasted into application windows. If formatting is critical (science/technical), submit as an attachment with a note.
- **Transcripts as Last Paper Link**: Transcripts are still mostly paper-only; all other materials should be submitted online when possible.
- **Incomplete Application Limbo**: Most programs will not distribute your application to reviewers until it is 100% complete. One late letter means everything waits — and potentially misses the effective deadline.
- **Reserved Slots**: Most programs reserve a small percentage of admissions for special circumstances (meets some but not all requirements). Exceptional fit can override standard metrics.

## Mental Models
- Ship in a bottle = rejection. Gratuitous extras signal poor judgment. Only send what directly supports your candidacy.
- Think of the admissions process as two separate applications: one to the graduate school, one to the department.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming the online check-off is accurate**: "Complete" in the system has failed students who were then denied. Always verify human-to-human.
- **Sending unusual materials in one format only**: Paper-only items may be invisible to remote reviewers; digital-only may be missed if printed for committee review. Use both.
- **Sending gratuitous extras**: Cookies, ships in bottles, crayon drawings — all real cases, all rejections.
- **Relying on the admissions coordinator to route unusual material**: They may not log it into your file.

## Key Takeaways
1. Know whether your target program is a strong-graduate-school or strong-department institution, and direct your outreach accordingly.
2. For anything unusual, submit in both electronic and paper formats, directly to likely committee members.
3. Verify every component is received — do not trust automated completion checks.
4. Draft essays offline; pasting into windows is a one-way gate with no drafts to clean up.
5. Incomplete applications stall in limbo; one late letter delays everything.
