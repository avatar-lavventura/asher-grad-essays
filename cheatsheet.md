# Quick Reference — Graduate Admissions Essays (Asher, 4th Ed.)

---

## The Asher Application Sequence

| Step | Chapter | Do |
|------|---------|-----|
| Should you go? | Ch 1 | Check motivations against good/bad reasons list |
| Build school list | Ch 2 | 20+ programs nationally; rank on 5 dimensions |
| Activity log | Ch 3 | Per-school checklist with target dates; first-choice first |
| Pre-writing | Ch 5 | Complete ALL exercises before writing a word |
| First draft | Ch 6 | Write from heart; RTGDQ; no editing during writing |
| Edit draft | Ch 7 | Academizer pass → Reader's-brain pass |
| Letters | Ch 9 | Approach early; provide portfolio; waive rights; follow up weekly |

---

## Essay Quality Filter (should trigger a rewrite)

- Opens with "I always wanted to be a ___"
- Wrong school name appears anywhere
- Activities all appear brand new (suspicious clustering)
- Praises the program without naming specific professors
- Reveals character weaknesses (sloth, dishonesty, arrogance)
- Essay is longer than necessary (shows lack of discipline)
- Doesn't answer the question directly
- Same essay recycled without school-specific adaptation
- Claims of altruism not backed by anything else in the application

## Good Reasons vs. Warning Signs (Should You Go?)

| Good Reasons to Go | Warning Signs |
|---|---|
| Passionate interest in narrow topic | Going to please someone else |
| Vocational credential requirement | Clueless about a subject |
| Earning potential ($400K–$1.4M lifetime boost) | Secretly avoiding job market |
| Career advancement (credentials for mobility) | |
| Quality of life (lockstep with life satisfaction) | |
| Research interest | |
| Natural aptitude for school | |

## Funding Counterintuitions

| Common Belief | Asher's Correction |
|---|---|
| Expensive schools cost more | Often cheaper — deeper endowments |
| PhD costs more than master's | Often cheaper — PhD candidates better funded |
| Part-time is more affordable | Often not — you miss the salary boost and still pay interest |
| Wealthy students skip aid | Always apply — merit fellowships build academic credentials |

## Letter Writer Selection Grid

| Criterion | Evaluate |
|---|---|
| Knows your work | Can they write with detail and specificity? |
| Will say great things | Ask them directly what they'll say |
| Is reliable | Will they submit on time without heroic prompting? |
| Avoid | Celebrity letters, political letters, "friendly" professors who don't know your scholarship |
| Waiver | Always waive your rights (Buckley Amendment) |

---

## Opening Types (by strength)

1. Epiphany — specific place, sensory detail, the moment of decision
2. Self-definition in one sentence — "I am the sixth of seven children…"
3. Bold position — opens with a claim that promises payoff
4. Vivid specific detail — proves seriousness through scene
5. Do NOT use: "I always wanted to be a ___"

---

## School Evaluation Dimensions

1. Academics (faculty research, intellectual fit)
2. People (faculty attitude, current student culture)
3. Locale
4. Cost / funding
5. Admissibility

## Early Application Timeline

| Target Program Type | Apply This Early |
|---|---|
| Highly competitive programs | 2+ months before deadline |
| Typical programs | 30 days minimum |
| Never | Last 10 days (50% of students; at a disadvantage) |

## Aid Type Summary

| Aid Type | Source | Work Required | Who Gets It |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teaching Assistantship | Department | Yes (teach undergrads) | Competitive applicants |
| Research Assistantship | Specific professor | Yes (lab/research) | Applicants who impress one PI |
| Fellowship | School or third-party | No | Exceptional or underrepresented |
| Grant | External orgs | No | Field/background matching |
| Tuition Waiver | School | Sometimes | PhD / lab sciences (very common) |
| Loan | Federal/private | No | Anyone with a pulse + good credit |
