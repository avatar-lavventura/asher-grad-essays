# Glossary — Graduate Admissions Essays (Asher, 4th Ed.)

**Academizer** — Asher's name for the editing pass that converts first-draft casual language into graduate-level academic register (Ch 7)

**Activity Log** — Per-school checklist with target dates for every application task; the primary project-management tool for the application process (Ch 3)

**Admissibility** — One of the five school-evaluation dimensions; your realistic probability of acceptance given your profile (Ch 2)

**Assistantship** — Financial aid requiring work (teaching or research) in exchange for tuition waiver and stipend; separate application, earlier deadline (Ch 1)

**Bailout Master's** — Entering a PhD program primarily for funding but planning to exit with a master's; leaves a permanent stigma as "dropped out of PhD" (Ch 1)

**Buckley Amendment** — The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act; gives students the right to review letters of recommendation; should be waived (Ch 9)

**CHEA** — Council for Higher Education Accreditation; the authoritative database for checking if an institution is accredited (Ch 2)

**Department vs. Institution Rule** — At graduate level, only programs have reputations; evaluate the specific department, not the school as a whole (Ch 2)

**Epiphany Opening** — Essay opening that describes the specific moment the applicant decided on this career path, or first understood what it entailed; the most commonly effective essay structure (Ch 6)

**Essay Hall of Shame** — Asher's compilation of direct admissions-reader complaints; includes generic openers, recycled essays, sloppiness, wrong school name, and claims not backed by evidence (Ch 7)

**External Fellowship** — Third-party scholarship not administered by the applicant's target school; science applicants expected to apply for 3–4; finaid.org and fastweb.com are starting points (Ch 1)

**Fellowship** — Large award with no work requirement, reserved for exceptional or underrepresented candidates; comes in internal (from school) and external (third-party) varieties (Ch 1)

**Fixed-Date Admissions** — All applications reviewed at once after the deadline; reviewer fatigue still applies, earlier arrival is still safer (Ch 3)

**Front-Loading Aid** — When programs offer heavy first-year funding packages to win admissions competition, then reduce support in later years; always ask about year-to-year reliability (Ch 2)

**Grant** — Smaller fellowship; ranges from a few hundred to a few thousand dollars; same research process as fellowships (Ch 1)

**Happy Confluence** — Asher's term for the essay moment where the applicant's philosophy and a specific program's stated values or faculty interests meet (Ch 6)

**Internal Fellowship** — Aid awarded by the graduate school or department to which you're applying; discovered by inquiring with the program (Ch 1)

**Law of Descending Prestige** — In academic hiring, faculty typically land at institutions with less prestige than where they received their highest degree; prestige of your graduate program matters if you want an academic career (Ch 2)

**Narrative Exposition** — The labyrinthine academic prose style taught in high school; Asher recommends minimizing it in essays (Ch 6)

**Pool of Data** — The raw material generated by Chapter 5 exercises; most of it will not appear in the essay, but it produces the detail that distinguishes strong essays (Ch 5)

**Reach** — A program's demographic diversity metric (students from X states, Y countries); applicants from underrepresented states or regions have a geographic advantage (Ch 2)

**Reductionism** — Using numbered lists to show clarity of thought; efficient but tiring if overused; one or two per essay maximum (Ch 6)

**Rights Waiver** — Signing away the right to review recommendation letters; always advised because programs discount or refuse to distribute non-waived letters (Ch 9)

**Rolling Admissions** — Applications reviewed and decisions made as they arrive throughout the cycle; slots in the admit pool fill over time; early submission strongly advantageous (Ch 3)

**RTGDQ** — "Read The Gosh Darn Question"; Asher's rule to read the exact wording of each essay prompt before and during drafting (Ch 6)

**Strong Department Model** — Admissions authority held by the department; graduate school is administrative (Harvard example) (Ch 4)

**Strong Graduate School Model** — Graduate school sets binding standards and can override department decisions (Duke example) (Ch 4)

**Teaching Assistantship (TA)** — Tuition waiver + stipend in exchange for teaching undergraduate courses; prestigious for academic career paths; has separate, earlier application deadlines (Ch 1)

**Research Assistantship (RA)** — Funded position in a specific professor's lab; easier to win than TA (impress one person vs. whole department); GPA in specialty matters more than overall GPA (Ch 1)

**Three-Tier Admissions** — How admissions officers sort applicants: (1) definite admits, (2) maybes, (3) rejects; rolling admissions fills the first tier first (Ch 3)

**Tuition Waiver** — Common for PhD and lab science candidates; the published tuition rate is often fiction — no one has paid it in years in many departments (Ch 1)

**20-School Rule** — Start your school search with at least 20 programs nationally; you won't apply to all, but you need the breadth to build a quality shortlist (Ch 2)
